How privacy works

You never need to understand zero-knowledge proofs to play. But if you are curious, here is what the game actually guarantees, in plain English.

1

Your cards stay private

Your hand is committed on-chain, not published. The public chain holds only commitments — cryptographic fingerprints — of your hand, your plays, and the shuffle seed. Your opponent never sees your cards, and the chain itself never learns them.

2

Every round is proven, not promised

After each round a zero-knowledge proof lands on Midnight showing that every card played — yours and the computer's — was actually dealt, every challenge was ruled by the rules, and the score follows. Play a card you were not dealt and the proof simply fails; the transaction can never confirm. When a game ends, its full transcript is published so anyone can re-run the rules and check the on-chain result for themselves.

3

The chain only learns true or false

When a claim is challenged, the contract checks the hidden cards against the public claim and discloses exactly one thing: whether it was honest — or, in 5 Up 2 Down, how many of the claimed cards were lies. It does not reveal what the cards were, and it reveals nothing about the rest of either hand.

An honest note about proving. When you use our hosted proof server, that server processes your private cards while generating your proof. Advanced players can run their own proof server for full privacy. The game's fairness is enforced by the contract either way — this choice only decides who can see. A round in thirty seconds (Original)

  1. Deal. Both sides commit random entropy, the shared seed is revealed, and each hand is dealt from it inside the proof. Nobody chose the cards.
  2. Play and claim. On your turn, play one to four cards face down and declare the current required rank — "Two Kings". The cards can be anything.
  3. Accept or challenge. Your opponent either accepts (the pile grows, the rank advances) or calls "Proof or Bluff!".
  4. Resolve. A zero-knowledge proof reveals whether the claim was true. The pile is discarded and points are awarded. First to the mode's target score wins.
